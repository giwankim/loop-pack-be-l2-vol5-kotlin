# Naming the on-hand stock count: `stock` vs `stockQuantity`

Researched 2026-10-08 from primary sources only: source files pinned to a release tag or commit, or the vendor's own API reference. Every table row links to the file or page that was fetched.

## Answer

Neither name is the majority choice. Across 22 codebases, the most common shape is a dedicated stock or inventory entity whose count is a bare `quantity` or `qty` (7 of 22: Magento, PrestaShop, Saleor, Odoo, Bagisto, Shopizer, JPetStore). Next come state-qualified names on entities that hold more than one number (6 of 22: Sylius `onHand`/`onHold`, Spree and Solidus `count_on_hand`, Vendure `stockOnHand`/`stockAllocated`, commercetools `quantityOnStock`/`availableQuantity`, Medusa `stocked_quantity`/`reserved_quantity`). This project's case is a count held directly on the product. Eight codebases do that, and 6 of the 8 use a two-word compound: WooCommerce `stock_quantity`, nopCommerce `StockQuantity`, BigCommerce `inventory_level`, Shopify `inventoryQuantity`, eShop `AvailableStock` and Broadleaf `quantityAvailable`. Only 2 use one word: Shopware `stock` and OpenCart `quantity`. So `stockQuantity` is idiomatic: two widely deployed platforms use it word for word. In both, though, it sits among `stock_*` siblings (`stock_status`, `manage_stock`, `low_stock_amount`; `DisplayStockQuantity`, `MinStockQuantity`, `DisplayStockAvailability`). The head noun separates the count from those statuses, flags and thresholds, and both platforms reuse the same compound for reserved or per-warehouse amounts. The language or framework does not decide the choice. What decides it is what else lives on the entity:
- A dedicated stock entity lets the field drop the word "stock".
- More than one number calls for a qualifier.
- A family of `stock*` siblings pushes the count into a compound.

Plain `stock` is the rarest name (1 of 22), but it is the closest match to this project. Shopware's 2023 ADR merged its two numbers (`stock`, `available_stock`) into "only one field `stock`", lowered when an order is placed. That is exactly a single remaining-sellable count with no reservation ([Shopware ADR 2023-05-15, L15–L21](https://github.com/shopware/shopware/blob/v6.7.15.1/adr/2023-05-15-stock-api.md?plain=1#L15-L21)). This project's product has no `stock*` sibling and no second quantity. Its glossary names the concept 재고 (stock) and lists 재고량/재고 수 as words to avoid. So the conditions that pushed WooCommerce and nopCommerce to `stock_quantity` are absent here, and `stock` fits.

## 1. Frequency summary

This summary classifies each of the 22 codebases by the name of its **primary** stock count, which is the number the storefront sells against or decrements. Solidus is counted separately from Spree even though it is a fork. Row numbers refer to the table.

|---|---|---|
| Bare `stock` | **1** | Shopware (#9) |

| Bare `quantity` / `qty` on a dedicated stock / inventory / availability entity | **7** | Magento (#5 `qty`, #6 `quantity`), PrestaShop (#7), Saleor (#13), Odoo (#22), Bagisto (#27), Shopizer (#20, `productQuantity`, column `QUANTITY`), JPetStore (#29) |

## 2. What explains the choice: testing the hypothesis

The hypothesis had three parts. The evidence confirms two and partly refutes the third.

**(a) "Entities that hold several quantities qualify each name."** Mostly confirmed. Eleven entities store more than one stock number: rows 2, 7, 10, 13, 14, 15, 16, 21, 22, 25 and 26. In every one, the *secondary* numbers are qualified: `reserved_quantity`, `quantity_allocated`, `onHold`, `stockAllocated`, `ReservedQuantity`, `reserved`, `availableQuantity`, `incoming_quantity`. The *primary* number is qualified in 8 of the 11 (`on_hand`, `onHand`, `stockOnHand`, `stocked_quantity`, `quantityOnStock`, `available`, `StockQuantity`, `qty_available`). It is left as bare `quantity` in 3: Saleor `Stock`, PrestaShop `StockAvailable` and Odoo `stock.quant`. All three are dedicated stock entities, so the type name supplies "stock" and the unqualified field reads as "the" quantity.

**(b) "Entities that hold one number use a short name."** Confirmed on dedicated stock entities, refuted on the product.
- *Dedicated stock entity, one number:* 5 of 7 use a bare `qty`/`quantity`: Magento `StockItem.qty`, MSI `SourceItem.quantity`, Bagisto `qty`, JPetStore `qty`, Shopizer `QUANTITY`. The exceptions are Spree and Solidus `count_on_hand`.
- *Product / variant / SKU, one number:* only 2 of 8 use one word: Shopware `stock` and OpenCart `quantity`. The other 6 use a compound: `stock_quantity`, `StockQuantity`, `inventory_level`, `inventoryQuantity`, `AvailableStock`, `quantityAvailable`. On the product the name has to carry the "stock" idea itself, and most codebases add a head noun (quantity, level) or a qualifier (available) even when nothing competes with it. eShop is the clearest case: `AvailableStock` is the only number, and its comment reads plainly "Quantity in stock" ([CatalogItem.cs L28–L29](https://github.com/dotnet/eShop/blob/dc7ea499cd356924fb6689b3702964a5869dbae9/src/Catalog.API/Model/CatalogItem.cs#L28-L29)).

**(c) "A `quantity` field usually sits on a dedicated stock/inventory entity where 'stock' is already the type name."** Confirmed. Eight of the 9 bare `quantity`/`qty` fields sit on such an entity. The one exception is OpenCart's `product.quantity`. PrestaShop once kept `quantity` on `Product` and has deprecated it in favour of `StockAvailable::$quantity` ([Product.php L67–L72](https://github.com/PrestaShop/PrestaShop/blob/9.2.0/classes/Product.php#L67-L72)). Magento's newer MSI also spells the column out (`qty` → `quantity`) while keeping it bare ([SourceItemInterface L27](https://github.com/magento/inventory/blob/1.2.9/InventoryApi/Api/Data/SourceItemInterface.php#L27)).

**An extra factor the hypothesis missed: a `stock*` sibling.** No codebase that has a stock *status* field next to the count names the count bare `stock`:
- WooCommerce has `stock_status` beside `stock_quantity`.
- OpenCart has `stock_status_id` beside `quantity`.
- nopCommerce has `DisplayStockAvailability`, `DisplayStockQuantity` and `MinStockQuantity` beside `StockQuantity`.
- BigCommerce has `inventory_tracking` and `inventory_warning_level` beside `inventory_level`.

Shopware, the one bare-`stock` codebase, signals availability through a boolean named `available` and has no `stock*` status field. When "stock" already prefixes a status, flag or threshold, the count takes a head noun so that `stock_*` names a family and `stock_quantity` names its number.

**What did *not* predict the choice: language or framework.** PHP alone covers `stock` (Shopware), `stock_quantity` (WooCommerce), `quantity` (PrestaShop, OpenCart), `qty` (Magento, Bagisto) and `onHand` (Sylius). .NET covers both `StockQuantity` (nopCommerce) and `AvailableStock` (eShop). Java covers `quantityAvailable` (Broadleaf), `productQuantity` (Shopizer) and `qty` (JPetStore).

## 3. Is `stockQuantity` idiomatic?

Yes, but it belongs to a context. Two of the 22 codebases use it as their primary name: WooCommerce `stock_quantity` (#3) and nopCommerce `StockQuantity` (#24). Its use goes with three things:

1. **A flat product model with a family of `stock*` siblings.** WooCommerce's product props run `manage_stock`, `stock_quantity`, `stock_status`, `backorders`, `low_stock_amount` ([L83–L87](https://github.com/woocommerce/woocommerce/blob/11.2.0/plugins/woocommerce/includes/abstracts/abstract-wc-product.php#L83-L87)). nopCommerce's `Product` runs `StockQuantity`, `DisplayStockAvailability`, `DisplayStockQuantity`, `MinStockQuantity`, `LowStockActivityId` ([L285–L330](https://github.com/nopSolutions/nopCommerce/blob/release-4.90.8/src/Libraries/Nop.Core/Domain/Catalog/Product.cs#L285-L330)).
2. **Other `*Quantity` fields nearby.** nopCommerce `Product` also has `OrderMinimumQuantity` and `OrderMaximumQuantity` ([L345–L350](https://github.com/nopSolutions/nopCommerce/blob/release-4.90.8/src/Libraries/Nop.Core/Domain/Catalog/Product.cs#L345-L350)), and `ProductWarehouseInventory` pairs `StockQuantity` with `ReservedQuantity` ([L18–L26](https://github.com/nopSolutions/nopCommerce/blob/release-4.90.8/src/Libraries/Nop.Core/Domain/Catalog/ProductWarehouseInventory.cs#L18-L26)).
3. **Reuse as a generic "amount of stock" across entities.** WooCommerce names the reserved amount in `wc_reserved_stock` `stock_quantity` too ([L2064–L2072](https://github.com/woocommerce/woocommerce/blob/11.2.0/plugins/woocommerce/includes/class-wc-install.php#L2064-L2072)). nopCommerce reuses `StockQuantity` on both `Product` and `ProductWarehouseInventory`. In these codebases the compound is a reusable unit of vocabulary, not a field-specific qualifier.

It does **not** go with PHP or WordPress specifically: nopCommerce is .NET, and most PHP codebases in the sample use something else (see §2). One detail: WooCommerce's storage key is bare `_stock`. The compound appears only at the object/API layer, where it sits beside `stock_status` ([data store L478–L479](https://github.com/woocommerce/woocommerce/blob/11.2.0/plugins/woocommerce/includes/data-stores/class-wc-product-data-store-cpt.php#L478-L479)).

Plain `stock` is rarer (1 of 22) but just as deliberate. In Shopware's own words, "Stock is stored as two distinct values: stock and available stock … This is unnecessarily complex," and the decision was "only one field `stock` in the product definition which always has a real time calculated value." It kept `availableStock` only as a write-protected mirror "because many integrations rely on this field" ([ADR L15–L19](https://github.com/shopware/shopware/blob/v6.7.15.1/adr/2023-05-15-stock-api.md?plain=1#L15-L19), [L227–L229](https://github.com/shopware/shopware/blob/v6.7.15.1/adr/2023-05-15-stock-api.md?plain=1#L227-L229)).

## 4. Naming-convention guidance from primary sources

- **Swift API Design Guidelines**, "Omit needless words": "Every word in a name should convey salient information at the use site. … those that are redundant with information the reader already possesses should be omitted. In particular, omit words that merely repeat type information." The counterweight in the same section is "Include all the words needed to avoid ambiguity." ([swift.org](https://www.swift.org/documentation/api-design-guidelines/)) Applied here: `Quantity` earns its place only when something on the entity could be confused with the count, such as a stock status or a second quantity. With `stock: Int` and no such sibling, the suffix restates that the value is a number, which `Int` already says. Whether that counts as "merely repeating type information" is a judgement call: `Quantity` is a role word, not the type name.
- **.NET Framework Design Guidelines**, "Names of Properties": "✔️ CONSIDER giving a property the same name as its type" (`public Color Color`). This supported the earlier `stock: Stock` value-object shape. The page's only explicit anti-suffix rule covers collections: "name collection properties with a plural phrase … instead of using a singular phrase followed by 'List' or 'Collection'." The page notes it reprints the 2008 second edition and "may be out-of-date" ([learn.microsoft.com](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/standard/design-guidelines/names-of-type-members)).
- **Kotlin coding conventions** say only "avoid using meaningless words (`Manager`, `Wrapper`) in names", and only about class names. They do not cover property suffixes ([kotlinlang.org](https://kotlinlang.org/docs/coding-conventions.html)).

## 5. Application to this project

Facts from the brief: one number on `Product`, the remaining sellable quantity, never below 0, no reservation, no `stockStatus`, and a glossary that names 재고 (stock) and avoids 재고량/재고 수.

- The shape matches Shopware after its 2023 ADR: one number on the product, lowered at order placement, with no reserved or available split. Shopware calls it `stock`, with column `stock`.
- None of the conditions that produced `stock_quantity` elsewhere is present: no `stock*` status, flag or threshold sibling, no second quantity, and no other entity that reuses the term.
- The glossary already rules that the count *is* "stock". Adding `Quantity` would bring the avoided 재고량 back into the code.
- `stock` is not a MySQL 8.4 keyword (the list runs `STATUS`, `STOP`, `STORAGE`, `STORED` …), so the column `stock` needs no quoting ([MySQL 8.4 keywords](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/keywords.html)).
- Mutator names already use "stock" as the noun in two codebases: eShop has `AddStock` / `RemoveStock` ([CatalogItem.cs L62, L86](https://github.com/dotnet/eShop/blob/dc7ea499cd356924fb6689b3702964a5869dbae9/src/Catalog.API/Model/CatalogItem.cs#L62-L86)), and Saleor has `increase_stock` / `decrease_stock` ([models.py L541–L551](https://github.com/saleor/saleor/blob/3.23.40/saleor/warehouse/models.py#L541-L551)). That matches a `stock` property.
- Revisit the name when a reserved/allocated number or a stock status arrives. The evidence then points to one of two moves: qualify every number on the product (Sylius `onHand`/`onHold`), or move the numbers to a dedicated stock entity with a bare primary `quantity` and qualified secondaries (Saleor, PrestaShop, Odoo).
